# Physical Review Letters venue profile

Use this profile only when the target journal is Physical Review Letters.
Recheck the current PRL scope, author guidance, article limits, and editorial
policies before treating any operational detail as current. The links below
were last checked on 2026-09-01.

## Reader and article contract

PRL addresses physicists across subfields rather than only the paper's immediate
specialty. Its introduction must therefore make the issue, principal advance,
and warranted significance recoverable before specialist machinery dominates.
Broad readership does not mean the general public, and it does not license an
opening broader than the evidence-supported question.

Use the current official sources:

- [PRL information for authors](https://journals.aps.org/prl/authors)
- [Physical Review editorial policies](https://journals.aps.org/authors/editorial-policies)
- [PRL editorial policies and practices](https://journals.aps.org/prl/authors/publish/policies-and-practices-physical-review-letters-july-2013)
- [The Aim of a Good Introduction](https://journals.aps.org/edannounce/PRLv95i17.html)
- [Successful Letters](https://journals.aps.org/prl/edannounce/PhysRevLett.100.100001)

Treat historical editorials as design evidence, not as current submission rules.
In particular, do not import their historical length guidance without checking
the current journal requirements.

## Introduction obligations

Before field-specific notation or machinery, let the intended PRL reader recover:

1. the physics issue or unresolved relation;
2. the established state needed to assess it;
3. the concrete advance reported by the paper;
4. why that advance changes understanding or capability beyond the immediate
   model or technique; and
5. the material scope and limits of that consequence.

Explain significance through the actual change produced by the result. Prestige,
novelty alone, a claim of broad interest, or an assertion that a topic is timely
does not establish PRL-level importance. Keep the introduction distinct from the
editor-facing submission justification: each must be supported, but they serve
different readers and functions.

## PRL audit

Reject or revise the introduction when:

- a physicist outside the immediate subfield cannot identify the active issue
  before specialist terms accumulate;
- the opening substitutes a universal physics claim for a narrower supported
  consequence;
- closest prior work or competing approaches are missing or mis-scoped;
- the principal achievement is expressed only as a method, calculation, or
  novelty claim without its physics consequence;
- a Letter's compression removes the premise needed to understand why the
  result matters; or
- historical editorial advice is presented as a current rule without checking
  the present journal guidance.
