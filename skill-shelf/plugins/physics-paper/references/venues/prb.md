# Physical Review B venue profile

Use this profile when the target journal is Physical Review B. Other Physical
Review journals have different scientific communities, article types, and
acceptance criteria; consult their own current pages. Check the current PRB
pages when submission is imminent; the links below were verified on
2026-09-21.

## Official venue contract

Physical Review B asks that a manuscript:

- make a high-quality, significant contribution in a specific area and generate
  interest for PRB readers;
- be understandable to a broad spectrum of researchers;
- avoid unfamiliar terminology unless it carries a necessary precise meaning;
  and
- place its key results in the relevant literature.

PRB Regular Articles have no length limit and are intended to give authoritative,
substantive accounts. PRB Letters are limited in length and require important
results in an accessible short form. Interpret general readership as the wide
condensed-matter and materials-physics readership served by PRB, not all
scientists and not the general public.

- https://journals.aps.org/prb/about

## Consequences for an introduction

The introduction must let a reader outside the immediate subfield answer four
questions before specialist machinery dominates:

1. What physical phenomenon, observable, or obstruction is under study?
2. What does it reveal or make calculable in condensed-matter physics?
3. What was established before this paper, including any material boundary of
   calculational or conceptual control?
4. What concrete understanding or capability does this paper add?

Meet these obligations with physics, not promotional adjectives. Significant
does not license claims that a topic is central, timely, broad, or widely
studied without evidence. Explain significance through a consequence: a
universal quantity becomes calculable, two descriptions become distinguishable,
a numerical observation acquires a field-theory origin, or an assumption that
controlled earlier work is removed.

For a Regular Article, retain enough context to connect neighboring
communities, define the paper's objects, and distinguish the main contribution
from its checks and extensions. For a Letter, compress this entry ramp while
preserving the same logical obligations. In addition, establish from the paper
and current literature why the result is of current interest and why the
advance is significant to the field. Accessibility and technical novelty alone
do not meet the Letter threshold.

## Physical Review audit

Reject the introduction if any answer is no:

- Can a broad PRB reader locate the problem
  before encountering model-specific notation or an unfamiliar formalism?
- Does each piece of broad context materially locate, distinguish, connect, or
  reorient the scientific problem, or change the reader's understanding of the
  subject, prior state of knowledge, or consequence of the result?
- Is the main advance expressed as new physical understanding, empirical
  evidence, materials or experimental capability, or calculational control
  rather than novelty alone?
- Are the closest prior results stated at source-supported strength, including
  any limit attributed to them?
- Are specialized terms introduced because the argument needs them, with their
  physical role stated before technical detail?
- For a Letter, does the introduction make a body- and source-grounded case for
  both the current interest of the result and the significance of the advance
  to the field?

Broad accessibility does not override the outline-leakage gate. A generic field
opening, an invented historical progression, or a topic presented merely as a
setting remains unacceptable.
