# Exposition for a study note

Adapted for study notes from the `academic-writing:exposition` pedagogical-exposition workflow. Use when drafting or substantially restructuring a teaching passage, worked example, derivation, or chapter sequence. The note should stand alone for the intended reader; its order must be supported by the subject, not by the author's list of tasks.

## Set the learning contract

Identify the reader's starting knowledge from the project's audience statement and the text itself. Do not infer expertise from the difficulty of an existing draft. State internally what the reader should be able to explain, calculate, compare, or predict after the passage. Topic-specific mechanisms remain learning targets even when the reader knows the basic mathematics. Preserve source claims, conditions, approximation regimes, citations, and the strength of conclusions. Missing scientific premises require evidence or calculation, not smoother prose.

Before asking the reader to retain unfamiliar preparation, give an intelligible object, discrepancy, or question. A list of future technical topics does not establish a reason to learn them. A question mark alone does not create a live question: the preceding result or setup should make it arise. Do not add a compulsory motivation sentence before each definition. The reason may already be visible from the preceding calculation.

## Build usable intermediate understanding

Read in order, using only the reader's licensed baseline and what has appeared so far. At each transition, ask what operation the next step requires: its inputs, the relation combining them, and the resulting distinction or conclusion. A definition establishes a meaning but may not yet establish how to use it. A named principle does not compress unfamiliar relations merely by being named.

When the transition fails, locate the cause before editing:

- The purpose is not yet intelligible: establish the situation or unresolved question.
- The needed relation is not usable: work through the connection or a representative case.
- Inputs are available but hard to retrieve together: change order, representation, notation, or local restatement. Use the [working-memory review](working-memory-review.md).
- The inference lacks a true premise: obtain evidence, perform the calculation, or state the result as an external theorem with its relevant conditions.

Complete meaningful stages. A calculation, example, or preparatory section should leave a result the reader can use next. When branching into prerequisites, give the branch a local purpose and return to the main argument without requiring reconstruction of the entire earlier chain. Do not impose a universal example-before-definition order or a fixed section template. A long proof may be the clearest route when its inputs remain available.

Keep the visible reading route sufficient with disclosures and equation hints closed. Optional details may hold the proof of a clearly stated input, but not the object, premise, condition, or result required for the next inference. A figure should appear before or beside the inference it makes easier; use the main skill's visualization test.

## Verify the repaired sequence

Replay the changed span from the same starting knowledge. For each substantive change, record outside the finished note: the old bottleneck, what the revised text now lets the reader do, how that result supports the next step, and which scientific conditions were preserved. Test the smallest downstream use, not merely the new definition or opening sentence. An expert review of text is not evidence that a human reader has learned it.
