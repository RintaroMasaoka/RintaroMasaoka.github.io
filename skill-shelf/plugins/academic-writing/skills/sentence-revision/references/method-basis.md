# Method basis

This skill combines established revision practices but does not treat any one
of them as a complete procedure for research-paper prose.

- Flower and Hayes model composing as recursive goal-directed processes rather
  than a linear prewrite--write--rewrite sequence. This supports reopening
  higher-level decisions when a local edit exposes a planning defect: Linda
  Flower and John R. Hayes, “A Cognitive Process Theory of Writing,” *College
  Composition and Communication* **32**, 365--387 (1981),
  [accessible copy](https://www.lirvin.net/1301sitebb/FlowerHayesCognitiveProcessTheory81.pdf).
- Sommers distinguishes the global, recursive revisions of experienced writers
  from local replacement strategies. This supports the null-sentence test and
  the rule that lexical polishing cannot rescue a misplaced action: Nancy
  Sommers, “Revision Strategies of Student Writers and Experienced Adult
  Writers,” *College Composition and Communication* **31**, 378--388 (1980),
  [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ240356).
- Flower's distinction between writer-based and reader-based prose supports
  treating the public reader's available context as a design constraint rather
  than copying the order in which the author discovered the ideas: Linda
  Flower, “Writer-Based Prose: A Cognitive Basis for Problems in Writing,”
  *College English* **41**, 19--37 (1979),
  [accessible copy](https://atgender.eu/wp-content/uploads/sites/207/2022/03/Linda_1979.pdf).
- Reverse outlining assigns a main idea or purpose to each completed paragraph
  and tests logical progression. The present workflow applies a related test at
  sentence scale while retaining the paragraph as the governing unit:
  [George Mason University Writing Center](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/writing-resources/writing-as-process/reverse-outlining).
- Faigley and Witte distinguish surface changes from text-base meaning changes
  and further distinguish microstructural from macrostructural revision. This
  motivates recording the change class and returning structural changes to the
  calling section skill: Lester Faigley and Stephen Witte, “Analyzing
  Revision,” *College Composition and Communication* **32**, 400--414 (1981),
  [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.2307/356602).
- Gopen and Swan analyze how topic position, stress position, and given--new
  progression shape a reader's interpretation of scientific prose. These ideas
  motivate the information-structure and permutation tests; they are
  expectations to test, not inviolable templates: George D. Gopen and Judith A.
  Swan, “The Science of Scientific Writing,” *American Scientist* **78**,
  550--558 (1990), [authorized reprint](https://courses.ems.psu.edu/styleforstudents/print/c10_p6.html).
- Scientific revision corpora show that version-aligned edits can be recorded
  at document, sentence, and span levels together with edit intentions. They
  justify preserving inspectable revision units, but they do not determine
  which revision is rhetorically correct: Chao Jiang, Wei Xu, and Samuel
  Stevens, “arXivEdits: Understanding the Human Revision Process in Scientific
  Writing” (2022), [arXiv:2210.15067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15067).

The distinctive procedure here is the integration of four constraints in one
persistent record: a prospective reader-state transition, scientific warrant,
counterfactual necessity tests down to lexical choice, and synchronization with
the accepted manuscript sentence. The claim that this exact integrated method
is absent from the literature is not required for using it and should not be
made without a dedicated literature review.
