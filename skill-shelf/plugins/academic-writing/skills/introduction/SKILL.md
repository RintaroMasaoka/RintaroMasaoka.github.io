# Expository Introduction

Use `$physics-paper:introduction` for a physics research paper or preprint. For
research papers in other fields, use a discipline- and venue-appropriate
workflow rather than importing physics defaults. This skill serves
expository scholarly documents whose purpose is to organize or explain existing
knowledge for a reader.

Write an introduction whose account of why this work is being undertaken is warranted. Individually true sentences can still create a false account of a research problem through their selection, omission, or causal connection. Test that account before improving its fluency.

## Inputs and scope

Recover the reader's question and starting knowledge, document genre, requested operation, source material, and actual or planned body. Inspect supplied manuscripts and primary sources at their given paths or URLs. A named research topic alone does not establish its motivation. Ask a focused question only when plausible interpretations would produce materially different introductions and the available material cannot distinguish them.

Identify the understanding the reader wants to acquire. A note or review can
explain established work; it does not need a new research gap. An intrinsic
mathematical question can justify an exposition without an application or
historical narrative.

This skill selects and warrants the introduction's framing. It does not establish a new scientific result or replace detailed proof, citation, or pedagogical review. Follow any applicable review requirements for substantive teaching changes.

For study notes, reviews, and technical expositions, use the reader's learning
question without inventing research novelty. For a narrowly requested prose
inspection, `$academic-writing:prose-review` supplies selected perspectives.

## Establish the account before drafting

Make a compact working record outside the deliverable, using only the distinctions that affect this task:

- What object or situation is under discussion, and what does the reader want to know about it?
- What is already established, and what difficulty or unresolved relation remains relevant here? Distinguish a research problem from something merely unknown to this reader.
- What does the target work actually construct, explain, or establish, under which assumptions?
- What part of that work will this document deliver, and how does that answer the reader's question?

### Keep relevance separate from warrant

The target document’s contribution determines which background is relevant to include. It does not provide evidence about the external subject. In particular, relevance to the document does not establish that selected topics are central, representative, naturally grouped, historically connected, converging toward the present question, or recognized as an important gap.

For each consequential background move, distinguish:

1. the facts established by sources;
2. the relations or evaluations asserted or implied by their selection, omission, ordering, and connectives; and
3. the editorial reason this document discusses them.

The third item licenses inclusion, not the first two. Every material relation or evaluation in the external account needs source evidence or an explicit source-grounded argument. Citations supporting separate facts do not by themselves support their grouping, causal connection, historical progression, collective importance, or the claim that one motivates another.

State editorial choices in the author’s voice, such as "we focus on" or "this raises the question we study." If the document itself establishes a relation, present it as a question, contribution, or conclusion. Do not insert it into the prior account as though it had independently organized the field.

Apply this test to the passage as a whole, not only to individual sentences: remove the target document’s desired story, contribution, and results as premises. The cited evidence and any explicit argument in the passage must still support every claim it makes about taxonomy, consensus, significance, motivation, and historical trajectory. Otherwise recast the unsupported content as document scope or as something to be established.

Inspect the body when available. If only a plan exists, describe a planned route and avoid claiming completed derivations or proven coverage. When the sources leave a material uncertainty, narrow or qualify the account; do not supply a plausible story to make the introduction feel complete. Bring unresolved choices to the user when they determine the document's purpose.

## Choose the entry point

Start from a problem, object, phenomenon, or contrast the intended reader can recognize. Select background because it changes their understanding of this particular task: what was possible, what is difficult, what the new construction changes, or why the chosen calculation bears on the question.

Preserve the difference between:

- the historical motivations documented for a researcher;
- the uses and consequences of their work;
- the reason this document chooses to study it.

These can overlap, but one is not evidence for another. Biographical facts belong when they illuminate a relevant intellectual setting. A researcher's prestige, a field's importance, or a list of neighboring topics cannot carry the explanation of relevance.

Do not impose a broad-to-narrow funnel or a background–gap–contribution template. A focused question may support a short, direct opening. A historical opening is appropriate when the historical relation is itself part of what the reader needs to understand.

## Write the substantive connection

Give enough content for the reader to see why the next claim follows. Naming a difficulty is not explaining it: when an obstacle motivates the document, identify what attempted operation fails, what condition is hard to preserve, or what comparison remains unresolved. Scale the detail to the introduction; the proof may come later.

An example should instantiate the relevant relation. A conditional assertion such as “if the result were independent of the choice, it would define an invariant” can state a criterion, but cannot stand in for an example in which the criterion is actually met. Either identify the relevant object and justified property, or present the assertion explicitly as the task to be solved.

Preserve the roles of assumptions and choices. In mathematical physics, changing physical data, changing auxiliary computational data, and changing a representation are different operations. Existence, uniqueness, independence, and equivalence also make different claims. State the distinction when the motivation relies on it, rather than projecting a special theory's property onto an entire field.

Present the document's contribution at the level the body supports. Check that the announced method reaches the promised endpoint with the available inputs: reducing a task to another unsolved task does not complete it. When only the reduction is supplied, promise that reduction or identify the additional result needed. Connect selected examples or calculations to the mechanism they expose. State material limits where they affect the promised answer. Avoid turning every limit into a defensive disclaimer or claiming a universal advance from a useful reformulation.

## Check the account, then the prose

Reread the proposed introduction with its sources and body, checking three different questions:

1. **Warrant:** Are both its statements and the relations it implies supported? Examine consequential “therefore,” “however,” “to do this,” topic groupings, evaluative predicates, and historical transitions. Would the passage make the reader infer a broader scope, a different motivation, or a stronger novelty claim than the evidence permits?
2. **Independence:** After removing the target document’s desired story, contribution, and results as premises, do the cited evidence and explicit arguments still support its account of the field? If not, mark the unsupported material as editorial scope, a research question, or a result.
3. **Reader use:** Can the intended reader identify the question, why this approach addresses it, and what they will obtain from this document? Check the transition into the first substantive section using only information already available to them.

A smooth narrative and a recoverable purpose do not establish that the purpose is the right one. As a diagnostic, temporarily remove an opening generality: if the actual question and its rationale remain unchanged, consider shortening or replacing it. Do not use this as a ban on broad context that genuinely supplies orientation.

For independent review, when requested or required by the surrounding workflow, supply the reader's original question, the draft, relevant raw source passages, and the body or plan. Withhold the author's defense and expected verdict. Ask separately about framing fidelity and reading continuity; neither verdict substitutes for the other. State evidence limitations rather than treating a prose reviewer as a scientific authority.

## Output

For drafting or revision, deliver clean introduction prose in the requested manuscript format. Keep working records, diagnoses, rejected framings, and review commentary outside the introduction. Report the substantive change and any material unsupported claim that remains.

For review-only work, identify the exact claim or connection, what evidence it would require, what is available, and the smallest justified repair. Do not rewrite an unrelated body or manufacture missing historical facts to complete the review.
