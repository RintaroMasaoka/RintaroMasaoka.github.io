# Shared authoring contract

Recover the target passage, requested operation (draft, revise, review), document genre, intended reader, source authority, and relevant project conventions. Use the user's current instructions and governing document specification. Infer stable context from evidence; do not infer reader expertise from the difficulty of the existing draft. Ask narrowly only when an unresolved premise materially changes the output, and continue independent work.

Keep these authorities distinct: the document purpose governs selection and scope; scientific sources and checked calculations support claims; the reader baseline governs explanation depth; project records govern adopted notation and terminology. Read existing records rather than creating competing ledgers. If the audience changes, reconsider dependent prose and review conclusions.

Preserve mathematical meaning, assumptions, quantifiers, approximation regimes, citations, and the strength of conclusions. A missing scientific premise needs evidence, not smoother prose. Changes to notation or ordering must propagate to affected references and uses. Keep manuscript-specific records in the consuming project.

When drafting or substantively revising teaching prose, check that terms and symbols are identifiable at their point of use against the licensed reader baseline; apply the [term and symbol availability check](audit-reader-state.md#term-and-symbol-availability). Include the same check in reader-state reviews and explicit requests to inspect undefined vocabulary or notation. Scientific correctness and a terminology registry do not establish availability to the reader. Keep other targeted reviews within their requested scope.

Select only the work required by the request. A prose review does not imply exhaustive claim auditing or human terminology approval. A review request returns findings; an authorized writing request returns clean artifact-ready prose. Keep audit ledgers, rejected alternatives, production discussion, and repair history outside the delivered text. Report unresolved evidence and limits of verification separately.

The public skills have distinct outputs:
- `$academic-writing:introduction`: a warranted opening and statement of scope.
- `$academic-writing:abstract`: a source-faithful synopsis.
- `$academic-writing:exposition`: an explanation whose intermediate understanding supports later steps.
- `$academic-writing:prose-review`: selected prose findings or authorized revisions.
- `$academic-writing:figures`: a rendered, inspected figure with editable sources.
- `$academic-writing:terminology`: a project-owned term registry and enforcement results, when this governance workflow is requested.
- `$academic-writing:notation`: durable notation choices in the existing convention owner.
- `$academic-writing:claim-audit`: bounded or exhaustive claim accounting, evidence, and unresolved findings for a specified revision.

These are available capabilities, not a mandatory sequence. Whole-paper specification management, study-session pacing, HTML production, and animation remain the consuming workflow's responsibility. Independent author/reviewer contexts are an execution requirement where specified; packaging does not establish that separation.
