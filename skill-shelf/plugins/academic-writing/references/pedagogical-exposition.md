# Pedagogical Exposition

Use for scholarly explanations whose purpose is to teach: study notes,
textbook passages, tutorials, and instructional derivations. Infer the function
from the task and audience, not technical density or the word “academic”. A
research paper may contain a pedagogical passage; apply this workflow to that
passage without turning the whole paper into a lesson.

## Learning contract

Specify the reader's starting knowledge and the understanding the passage must
build: a relation they should explain, an operation they should reconstruct,
or a consequence they should predict. Topic-specific mechanisms remain learning
targets even when the reader knows the discipline's basic mathematics. Preserve
source claims, conditions, and scope; verify substantive scientific changes
through the appropriate subject workflow.

Apply the [audience-baseline procedure](audit-reader-state.md#apply-the-audience-baseline)
to the relationships that determine the passage's depth and order. It governs
when missing reader information requires a question, how known material affects
inclusion, and which draft and review decisions must be revisited when the
baseline changes. Use the same current baseline for drafting and verification.

Judge a learning sequence by whether understanding acquired along the way
supports the next step. Correct statements, defined terms, complete coverage,
and descriptions of future uses do not by themselves establish this progress.
The key editorial question is:

> Before asking the reader to retain unfamiliar information, has the text made
> its purpose intelligible, and does the ensuing explanation turn it into
> understanding the reader can use?

This is a design criterion for teaching, not an experimentally established
universal law of motivation. A research abstract can communicate contributions
to knowledgeable peers without building each concept. A teaching passage has
the additional obligation to build the understanding its later steps use.

## Establish motivation before a new learning demand

Motivation is part of the path into unfamiliar material. Establish an object,
question, discrepancy, or task the reader can understand and a reason to pursue
it before requiring a new body of definitions or preparation. The reason may
already follow from the preceding result or an accepted learning goal; it need
not be restated before each symbol, lemma, or sentence.

Check the reader's side of the connection. “This will be useful for X” does not
motivate the preparation when X is itself unintelligible. A list of future
technical topics offers no usable reason to retain their machinery. Conversely,
a question is not useful merely because it has a question mark: show the
situation that makes it a real question for this audience. An intrinsic
mathematical question can supply motivation without a practical application.

Preparation must have an intelligible local purpose even when the full
mechanism cannot yet be explained. For example, after posing how two measured
quantities can fluctuate together, establish what each variable and its spread
mean before teaching covariance. The learner can recognize what is being
measured while learning the components. Compare that with listing moments,
matrices, and decompositions solely because they will all be needed later.

Attend to the reader's emotional engagement through the explanation itself:
can they see why the work is worth doing, how to begin, and what counts as
progress? An unexplained detour can leave both purpose and attainability
uncertain. Ground the next step in something they can already recognize or do,
and let an actual intermediate result show progress. Generic reassurance,
praise, claims that a step is easy, or promises that everything will make sense
later do not supply that support. Report textual obstacles and their plausible
consequences; do not diagnose a particular reader's feelings from prose alone.

Motivation, emotion, and working-memory demands interact, but are distinct:
a well-motivated derivation can still require avoidable bookkeeping, and a
short, easy list can still give the learner no reason to study it. Difficulty,
uncertainty, and surprise can belong to learning when the question and a viable
next step remain available. The goal is sustained, informed engagement, not
constant reassurance or the removal of every struggle.

## Build usable understanding before relying on it

Track what each passage enables, not merely what it mentions. A definition can
establish a symbol's precise meaning while leaving its role, relations, and
conditions unfamiliar. Naming several facts as one “principle” does not make
them one familiar unit for the learner. Do not count named concepts as memory
slots or assume that a single exposure has created a durable mental schema.

At a suspected transition, use only the audience baseline and the text up to
that position. Briefly perform the operation the next step requires. Identify
its inputs, the relation used, and the resulting distinction or conclusion.
If this requires importing the later answer or the reviewer's topic expertise,
record the missing connection. This is a test of textual support, not proof
that a human reader has learned the material.

Locate the cause before choosing the repair:

- **Purpose not yet intelligible:** establish the situation or unresolved
  question that warrants learning the material. Moving a glossary closer is
  insufficient when the reason for learning it remains obscure.
- **Relations not yet usable:** work through the connection, comparison, or
  case that turns definitions into an operation. Stating its name or repeating
  the destination does not supply the mechanism.
- **Usable information hard to recover or combine:** change representation,
  notation, placement, or local restatement so the next inference's inputs can
  be used together. Apply [Audit working memory](audit-working-memory.md) when
  retention, suspended questions, or split attention need detailed inspection.
- **Actual warrant missing:** obtain or establish the needed mathematical or
  physical premise. A roadmap or motivating example cannot repair an invalid
  inference. An external theorem may be an appropriate input, but its relevant
  content and application still need to be intelligible here.

Repair the dependency that failed. Reordering a section, replacing a proof, or
changing notation may remove burdens that several explanatory sentences would
only describe. Keep significant mechanisms fully developed. Useful repetition
can connect representations or make a later inference possible; shortness and
non-repetition are not acceptance criteria.

## Complete meaningful stages

A stage may be a calculation, worked case, long proof, or preparatory account.
Its endpoint should give the learner something identifiable to use: for
example, which quantities change together, why a candidate construction fails,
or which assumption controls an approximation. Completion does not require a
summary box. Nor must all questions be answered immediately: a main question
can remain open while subsidiary results make attainable progress toward it.

When an explanation branches into prerequisites, check that each branch has a
reason the learner can understand and that returning to the main argument does
not require reconstructing all its earlier unfinished work. Preserve beneficial
component learning; teach recognizable properties or operations, then combine
them. An advanced theorem may precede a long proof when its problem, objects,
and claim are already intelligible. Do not force discovery of the proof's next
trick or impose a universal “example before definition” sequence.

Keep the main reading route sufficient for the learning inference with optional
foldouts, tooltips, links, and appendices closed. State the indispensable object,
premise, condition, or reported result where it is used. Reference calculations
and proofs of clearly stated inputs can remain in supplements. Apply the
[dedicated sequential review](audit-reader-state.md#dedicated-sequential-review)
to test the actual route, rather than accepting the presence of all equations
somewhere in the artifact.

## Verify the learning sequence

After changing the passage, replay the repaired span from its licensed starting
state. For each material finding, record outside the artifact:

- the current audience premise that governs this explanation's inclusion,
  including the evidence for treating its content as available or to be learned;
- the learning demand and the reason already available for undertaking it;
- the exact unresolved relations or separated inputs the old text required;
- a concrete operation the revised text now supports, with its premises;
- how its result supports the next step or closes a subsidiary question;
- any scientific claim, condition, or useful preparation that must be preserved.

A sentence promising progress is not evidence of progress. Check the smallest
meaningful downstream application, not only the added definition or equation.
When work was split among reviewers, the integrator must inspect transitions
between their ranges using what the earlier text actually establishes. Access
to the whole document or agreement among experts cannot substitute for this
check. For substantive teaching changes, use the dedicated sequential review above.
A reviewer unfamiliar with the drafting history is not necessarily a novice in
the subject; enforce the licensed reader baseline as well as the input boundary.

For diagnosis, give the exact bottleneck, the unavailable understanding, and a
concrete repair at the appropriate scale. For an edit, produce clean teaching
prose and keep this analysis outside it. Report review coverage and uncertainty;
textual simulation is not a learner study.

## Research basis and limits

The editorial procedures above synthesize these sources. Their experiments and
theories inform the design; they do not validate a universal test for advanced,
self-paced scholarly reading or guarantee a reader's emotional response.

- Bransford & Johnson (1972), [Contextual Prerequisites for Understanding](https://labs.la.utexas.edu/gilden/files/2016/04/Bransford_Johnson_1972.pdf), pp. 719–724: appropriate context before short spoken passages improved recall; the authors distinguish relational context from merely knowing a topic. In Experiment IV, the before/after comprehension-rating difference was not significant. Applied here: check that the reader has an interpretable situation, rather than count purpose sentences. These were not mathematical proof-reading or direct working-memory measurements.
- Cowan (2001), [The magical number 4 in short-term memory](https://memory.psych.missouri.edu/assets/doc/articles/2001/cowan-bbs-2001.pdf), §§1.2–1.3: chunks depend on associations in long-term memory, and capacity estimates require controlled conditions. Applied here: an expert's familiar unit can require several relations for a learner; names and definitions alone do not establish chunking. Do not assign a fixed slot budget to prose.
- Sweller (2010), [Element Interactivity and Intrinsic, Extraneous, and Germane Cognitive Load](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9128-5), abstract: the theoretical account distinguishes interacting demands essential to the task from those introduced by instructional procedures. Applied here: preserve the target reasoning while reducing bookkeeping imposed by its presentation. This source does not quantify the load of a particular passage.
- Mayer, Mathias & Wetzell (2002), [Fostering Understanding of Multimedia Messages Through Pre-training](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.8.3.147), abstract and publisher preview: learning components and their possible states before narrated explanations of brakes or a pump improved transfer in three experiments. Applied here: coherent preparation can help subsequent integration. The study does not establish that every advance definition helps, or that motivation can be omitted.
- Pekrun (2006), [The Control-Value Theory of Achievement Emotions](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~gratch/CSCI534/Old-Readings/Pekrun-control-value-theory.pdf), pp. 317, 326–327, 333–335: the framework connects perceived value and control with learning emotions and their effects on attention and engagement. Applied here: make purpose and attainable progress available. This is a theory-informed editorial inference, not a demonstrated emotional intervention for mathematical prose.
