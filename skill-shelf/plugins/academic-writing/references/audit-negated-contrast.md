# Audit Negated Contrast

Use this task when academic prose introduces a negated alternative, corrective
contrast, or anticipated misconception and its contribution to the reader's
understanding is uncertain. The problem is not negation itself. It is the use
of a rejected alternative to stage an explanation when the subject matter has
not made that alternative relevant.

## Inclusion test

Apply this task when the quality of a negative statement depends on its local
argumentative role. Typical candidates include:

- “not merely A,” “not A but B,” or “not all A” formulations whose comparison
  class or quantifier may be underspecified;
- sentences that tell the reader what they may think, expect, misunderstand,
  or find surprising before rejecting that response;
- negative claims in an introduction, heading, summary, or concluding box that
  may restage material already established in the derivation; and
- a contrast whose positive branch changes the topic instead of resolving the
  negated proposition.

Do not select or condemn a passage from a word such as “not,” “merely,”
“simply,” “only,” or their Japanese counterparts. A sentence that looks
formulaic in isolation may be required by the preceding calculation. A fluent
sentence may still introduce an alternative that exists only in the writer's
drafting process.

## Decision contract

Read the whole paragraph and the dependency-bearing part of the surrounding
section. Then identify four items:

1. **Negated proposition:** State precisely what is being rejected, including
   the operation, variables, quantifier, regime, and assumptions when they
   matter. If words such as “merely” or “all” cannot be expanded into a
   definite proposition, the contrast is too vague to assess or retain as
   written.
2. **License:** Locate what made that proposition a live candidate for the
   reader: a preceding equation, definition, observation, established term,
   explicit earlier claim, unavoidable disciplinary ambiguity, a misconception
   documented for the target readership, or a genuine scope expectation. Do
   not treat an imagined novice reaction or the writer's discarded
   interpretation as evidence.
3. **Resolution:** Locate the calculation, definition, condition, evidence, or
   exact positive proposition that decides the issue. A declaration of the
   chapter's “message,” “view,” “essence,” or importance does not by itself
   resolve a negated proposition.
4. **Consequence:** Determine what changes after the rejection: an assumption,
   domain of validity, notation, next calculation, admissible model, or reader
   contract, including an interpretation needed to apply an established result
   correctly. If removing the negative branch leaves both the reasoning and
   the reader's materially relevant interpretation unchanged, it is probably
   rhetorical staging or redundant recap.

A useful negation need not have an explicit affirmative sentence immediately
before it. A theorem may prove nonexistence; a calculation may make a term
vanish; a conventional name may create a real ambiguity; and a scope statement
may limit what the document promises. In each case the license and consequence
must still be identifiable.

## Diagnosis

Distinguish sentence-level and argument-level failures.

- **Required distinction:** The text makes two possibilities live and the
  negation selects between them. Retain the negation, making its object and
  conditions explicit when needed.
- **Unsupported contrast:** The rejected alternative has no license in the
  document or discipline. Remove it and state the supported proposition
  directly if that proposition is already established.
- **Vague contrast:** The rejected alternative could denote several materially
  different claims. Specify the actual operation and comparison before
  deciding whether negation is useful.
- **Unresolved contrast:** The text rejects one claim but follows with a slogan,
  metaphor, or adjacent topic rather than evidence for another. Return to the
  underlying argument. Do not manufacture a stronger positive sentence.
- **Redundant restaging:** A derivation has already established the point, but
  an introduction, heading, or summary recreates a naive alternative only to
  reject it. Keep the derivation and remove the staged correction unless it
  serves a separate navigation or scope function.

Treat reader-oriented phrases such as “one might wonder” in the same way. The
question is useful when a visible gap, apparent contradiction, uncontrolled
step, or evidential limitation has just arisen. Remove the attribution to the
reader when the concern is real but the reader psychology is unnecessary; state
the gap or condition itself.

## Repair

Choose the smallest repair supported by the argument:

1. Preserve a necessary negative result or distinction.
2. Make an underspecified negated proposition concrete by naming the operation,
   fixed quantities, conditions, or comparison class already supported by the
   text.
3. Delete a fabricated alternative and retain the established positive claim.
4. Replace a claim about the reader's reaction with the actual mathematical or
   evidential gap.
5. When the surrounding section has not established either branch, stop the
   prose repair and report that the argument must be reconstructed. Do not add
   a new summary or “message” to compensate.

## Boundaries

- Do not infer whether a human or an AI wrote the passage. This task evaluates
  function, not provenance.
- Do not impose affirmative wording as a general preference. Absence,
  nonexistence, incompatibility, exceptions, and limits are often the content.
- Do not require every rejected alternative to have been written verbatim
  earlier. Equations, terminology, and genuine scope expectations can license
  it.
- Do not use an anticipated misconception to display the writer's care or to
  make an established result sound deeper.
- Do not silently repair scientific content. If the positive branch, limit,
  or condition is not established, report the gap outside the artifact.
- Do not add a concluding message after the body has already made the
  inferential result available to the reader.

## Output

- **Edit:** clean artifact-ready prose with necessary negations preserved,
  unsupported staging removed, and no diagnostic labels or discarded branches
  left in the manuscript.
- **Review:** identify the exact negative span, its negated proposition,
  license, resolution, consequence, and whether the problem belongs to the
  sentence or to the surrounding argument.
