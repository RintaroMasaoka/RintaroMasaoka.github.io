# Audit Expository Progression

Use this task when academic prose appears to advance by completing the
writer's or teacher's explanatory tasks rather than by following dependencies
generated by the mathematical, physical, historical, or evidential object.
Editorial actions such as introducing, distinguishing, interpreting,
correcting, and summarizing are diagnostic signals, not the quality criterion.
The defect occurs when announcing those actions occupies the place of
information the intended reader needs, while adding reading effort without
making the subject more intelligible or a reading decision more determinate.
Assess what the passage enables the reader to understand, identify, infer,
or decide from the text at that position. A statement of the author's intention
does not establish that contribution. Useful orientation, motivation, and
recaps can contribute without adding a new scientific fact; they must expose
a relation or route the reader can actually use.

A further failure concerns how the passage presents its status. An author's
plan, reminder, or intention can be written as ordinary finished prose without
being identified as production material. Its placement then gives it the
appearance of an explanatory contribution although the reader receives only
a description of work to be done. This can conceal the absence of a definition,
reason, or connection and transfer its reconstruction to the reader. Diagnose
the mismatch between the passage's actual function and its presented status;
do not infer deliberate deception or the author's private drafting history.

Do not instruct the artifact to be “object-driven” or to avoid task-oriented
writing. Such instructions easily reappear as sentences about what the text
will or will not do. Keep the audit contract outside the artifact and test the
observable dependency of each candidate passage.

## Inclusion test

Select this perspective when one or more of the following patterns may be organizing
the prose. These are candidates for inspection, not lexical violations.

1. **Editorial-action labels.** A heading or topic sentence names what the
   writer or reader will do—read X as Y, distinguish two notions, see what is
   shared, understand why something matters—while leaving the relevant objects
   or relation unnamed or underdetermined.
2. **Manufactured inquiry.** A question, contrast, misconception, or reader
   reaction is introduced without a preceding result, ambiguity, failed step,
   disciplinary expectation, or documented audience difficulty that makes it
   live. A negated case may also implicate `audit-negated-contrast`; report that
   possible overlap to the coordinator without duplicating that perspective's
   proposition/license analysis unless that perspective is also assigned.
3. **Premature synthesis.** An introduction declares a unifying key, essence,
   correspondence, or chapter message before the required objects and
   conditions have been established, and later sections function primarily as
   demonstrations of that declaration.
4. **Symmetric task packaging.** Sections with materially different
   epistemic work are forced into a repeated sequence such as motivation,
   distinction, caveat, and takeaway; repeated boxes or parallel headings make
   the document look complete while hiding missing or unequal dependencies.
5. **Takeaway certification.** A conclusion, point box, or final sentence
   restates an inferential result as the “view,” “lesson,” “message,” or
   “essential point” of the section without changing a later inference,
   application, scope condition, or navigation decision.
6. **Instruction residue.** A drafting constraint appears in the artifact as
   content: for example, a sentence announces that the text will not focus on
   a task, will avoid a misunderstanding, or will now make a required
   distinction. This includes residue created while trying to suppress task
   orientation itself.
   Positive promises about audience preparation, accessibility, or future
   explanation belong in this scan too. They can repeat the author's work
   order even when they contain no prohibition or corrective contrast.
   Inspect whether such material is explicitly marked as a production note
   or is presented as finished exposition. Fluent sentences and reader-facing
   phrasing do not themselves convert a production intention into an
   explanatory contribution.

Do not select the perspective merely because prose contains a verb, a question, a
roadmap, first person, or an explicit purpose. Methods, proofs, protocols,
exercises, contribution statements, and long-document navigation can require
author or reader actions. A question can be fully subject-generated, and an
active heading can name the actual mathematical operation under study.

## Drafting prevention

When this perspective is selected for new drafting, recover a provisional dependency
spine before writing artifact prose. Keep it in working memory or review notes,
not in the artifact:

```text
object or setup
    -> result or constraint
    -> discrepancy, open choice, or required relation
    -> next definition, calculation, or claim
```

Draft from the spine rather than from a list of teaching objectives. The spine
may change when the derivation, source, or evidence changes. Do not stabilize
the original writing plan by converting contrary evidence into a caveat.

After drafting, run the cheap scan below before returning or writing the
artifact. Escalate only the flagged spans. This post-draft gate is required
when the perspective has been selected; a pre-draft instruction by itself is not a
verification.

## Cheap scan

Start with a low-cost scan. Read:

- the title and all headings;
- the first and last prose sentence of each section;
- abstract, scope, roadmap, conclusion, and boxed-summary material; and
- repeated labels or sentence frames used across sections.

For each candidate, answer only these questions:

1. **Actor:** Is the grammatical or implied actor the subject matter, or the
   writer, reader, document, chapter, or explanatory plan?
2. **Dependency:** Can the passage point to a preceding definition, equation,
   observation, conflict, evidential limitation, or established scope need
   that makes it necessary here?
3. **Contribution:** State concretely what the intended reader can newly
   understand, identify, infer, or decide because of this passage, and point
   to the wording and available context that enable it. “Sets expectations”
   or “helps the reader” is insufficient without specifying which expectation
   or decision becomes determinate. Include interpretive and navigational
   gains, not only new subject-level facts.
4. **Deletion:** If it is removed, does the subject-level reasoning and the
   reader's ability to follow it remain unchanged?

When a passage states an explanatory intention, identify the content that
would fulfill it and check whether that content is available where needed.
If it is available, test whether the announcement adds a usable route or is
redundant. If it is missing, distinguish deleting an empty announcement from
repairing the missing explanation; deletion alone cannot complete the latter.
An explanation provided later is not a defect merely because it is previewed:
test the preview's own contribution and the actual point of dependency.

Apply the contribution and deletion tests to prerequisite declarations and
roadmaps before treating them as necessary navigation. Identify the concrete
decision enabled by the sentence: where to start or skip, which external
result must already be available, or which assumptions limit a claim. A
promise to introduce concepts or assume little background does not establish
such a decision by itself. Distinguish an audience model used to design the
explanation from information the reader needs in the artifact; the former
should normally be realized through definitions and derivations in place.
Test whether that information is usable at the sentence's position:

- A prerequisite must identify knowledge or an operation sufficiently for the
  intended reader to assess readiness or find preparation. Broad field names
  qualified as “ordinary” or “basic” may leave both undecidable. Do not invent
  a syllabus to rescue an empty declaration; retain or specify prerequisites
  only where an actual dependency supports them.
- A preview must communicate a relation, purpose, or location interpretable
  from information already available. Listing unfamiliar technical names and
  promising to introduce them supplies neither their meaning nor their need.
  Test plausible knowledge states within the intended readership: what can
  a reader unfamiliar with the names infer, or what useful route or connection
  does a reader who knows them gain? Do not impose a novice audience on
  specialist prose. If the intended readers gain nothing beyond the author's
  intention to explain, remove the sentence. A preview need not
  teach every named concept, but must offer more than its name and a promise.

For example, “Only basic theory is assumed; the specialized concepts will be
introduced below” gives no determinate preparation or conceptual connection.
“The proof in section 3 uses the spectral theorem stated in section 2;
readers familiar with it can start at section 3” identifies an actual route
and can remain. An explicit prerequisite field in a course catalog likewise
serves a selection decision when its requirements are sufficiently specific.
Judge these functions in context; do not ban prerequisite statements or
first-person wording as a class.

The cheap gate passes when each candidate has a text-supported contribution
to understanding or a concrete reader decision, connected to the surrounding
argument or a genuine navigation need. Merely announcing an explanatory action
does not pass. Escalate flagged spans whose contribution cannot be specified
or whose repeated task-shaped framing substitutes for local structure, even
if the grammatical subject is impersonal. Keep the review local after a
single weak signal.

## Strong audit: recover the dependency spine

For each escalated span, reconstruct the smallest dependency spine that the
prose must expose:

```text
object or setup
    -> result or constraint
    -> discrepancy, open choice, or required relation
    -> next definition, calculation, or claim
```

The spine may be deductive, empirical, historical, or methodological. It need
not contain a literal question. Compare the candidate prose with this spine
and classify its function:

- **Subject-required move:** the object, evidence, or argument makes the move
  necessary. Retain it, even if it uses a question, first person, or a verb in
  the heading.
- **Necessary navigation:** the document's scale or genre requires a roadmap,
  scope boundary, or recap that helps the reader locate dependencies not
  visible locally. Name the reading, selection, or applicability decision it
  enables before retaining it. Placement in an introduction or a familiar
  textbook convention alone does not establish necessity. Retain the minimum
  statement that performs that job.
- **Editorial-task substitution:** an explanatory action stands where the
  actual objects and relation should be stated. Record whether it is openly
  presented as unfinished work or occupies the position of finished
  exposition, and whether the underlying explanation is absent or merely
  accompanied by a redundant announcement.
- **Manufactured inquiry:** the prose creates a question or rejected option so
  that the writer can answer it.
- **Premature synthesis:** the declared unity governs evidence selection or
  prevents later qualifications from revising the frame.
- **Template completion:** repeated structural slots create content that the
  local dependency spine does not require.
- **Redundant certification:** a result already available from the argument is
  reissued as a lesson or message.
- **Missing subject-level argument:** no defensible dependency spine can be
  recovered from the artifact. This is not a sentence repair.

When a unifying claim appears in an introduction, test whether later evidence
could force the document to narrow, revise, or abandon it. An abstract or
research-paper introduction may properly state a result early. The defect is
present when the declaration functions as a completion criterion and all
counterevidence is absorbed as caveat without changing the claim.

## Repair

Repair the earliest point where editorial organization displaced a
subject-level dependency.

1. Restore omitted definitions, fixed quantities, conditions, calculations,
   or observations when they are already supported by the source material.
2. Name the objects and the relation at issue. Do not merely convert a verbal
   heading into a noun phrase.
3. Let a real discrepancy or open choice generate the question. Remove a
   question whose only function was to start a planned explanation.
4. Remove repeated task templates and allow sections to have unequal shapes
   and lengths when their epistemic work differs.
5. Keep a summary only when it compresses separated results for a later use,
   changes the scope, or provides necessary navigation. Otherwise let the
   derivation carry its own conclusion.
   Apply the same rule to promises of self-containedness and prerequisite
   declarations. If they only report the author's teaching plan, delete them
   without replacing them with another promise. Preserve substantive
   assumptions and genuine references to prerequisites at their point of use.
6. If the opening thesis was not earned, do not replace it with a weaker
   slogan. Reconstruct the order from the dependency spine or report that the
   underlying research and argument must be completed first.
   Likewise, when a production intention stands in for missing exposition,
   supply the supported explanation or report the unresolved work outside the
   finished artifact. Do not make the passage appear complete by polishing
   the intention or labeling it a TODO inside a requested finished deliverable.

Never repair task orientation by adding sentences such as “we do not focus on
the task,” “rather than merely explaining,” “the discussion is
object-centered,” or “the point is not the summary.” These sentences preserve
the editorial task as the topic and leave correction residue in the artifact.

## Boundaries

- Task-oriented instructions are appropriate in the skill or work order. The
  audit concerns their leakage into an artifact whose subject is something
  else.
  Explicit TODOs and editorial notes in an authorized working draft disclose
  their status and are not this presentation failure. They may still indicate
  unfinished work. A substantive scope statement or a useful roadmap remains
  legitimate finished prose when it passes the contribution test.
- A procedural passage is not editorial merely because it describes an
  operation. The operation or proof step may itself be the subject matter.
- A textbook can use learning objectives, previews, and summaries. They must
  support navigation or later use rather than replace the local argument.
- A heading need not be nominal. Judge whether it identifies a determinate
  object-level operation or exposes a generic act of explanation.
- Do not infer AI authorship. The perspective diagnoses an organization of prose,
  not provenance.
- Do not preserve every feature of a human source as an authorial style. A
  source can contain its own unsupported roadmaps, imagined reader reactions,
  or redundant summaries.
- Do not silently invent scientific content to make a dependency spine look
  complete. Report the gap outside the artifact.

## Output

- **Edit:** return clean artifact-ready prose. Do not include audit labels,
  discarded tasks, reader-management commentary, or explanations of what the
  revision avoided.
- **Review:** identify the exact span, observed pattern, missing or present
  dependency, consequence, classification, and the smallest supported repair.
  Separate sentence-level repair from a section whose subject-level argument
  must be reconstructed.
