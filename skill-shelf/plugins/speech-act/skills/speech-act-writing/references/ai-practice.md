# Practice in the AI era

Reference IDs point to the [source map](sources.md). Distinguish experimental findings, practitioners' advice, and this package's proposals. Results can vary with the model, language, task, and interface. The literature has not validated this entire workflow.

## Define the work being delegated to AI

Research on AI co-writing reports outcomes involving authors' attitudes, emotional expression, creativity, and sense of authorship. Assuming that AI writes only facts would leave these changes unchecked. [A04–A08]

**This package's proposal:** Use the request and materials to decide whether the task is assistance with structure and expression, exploration of issues, proposals for claims, or critique. Preserve the author's position and strength of claims when assisting with expression. Exploration can introduce other positions, but distinguish them from claims the author has adopted. Do not add evaluations, experiences, agreement, or commitments while merely making prose smoother.

## Adapt to the recipient through the actual relationship

Dippold's qualitative study finds that a desire for efficiency and expectations of relational consideration can coexist in the same person. Nationality or a single attribute cannot fix a person's stylistic preferences. [A01]

Google's conversation-design documentation recommends using context already supplied, moving the current turn forward, and repairing specific trouble. This is practice for a particular voice platform; do not transfer its retry limits or similar rules to writing in general. [A02]

**This package's proposal:** Briefly specify the intended reader, reading purpose, authority, existing knowledge, and relationship with the author. Go beyond "for experts" or "make it natural." In an exchange, do not ask again about conditions already answered. Identify the uncertainty that blocks the next judgment. When a misunderstanding occurs, retain what was understood and change the explanation or question to address that particular point. Do not repeat the same vague paraphrase. [A11, A12]

## Distinguish the author's voice from the tone of the occasion

Mailchimp distinguishes relatively stable voice from tone that varies by situation, and prioritizes communication over compulsory fun. This is brand practice; do not import the brand's style itself into academic writing. [A03]

Hwang and colleagues' qualitative study connects authenticity with personal experience, position, agency, and participation in production, as well as stylistic similarity. [A06]

**This package's proposal:** Preserve the author's selected issues, characteristic expressions, emotional intensity, and concrete examples. "Make it academic" must not become an instruction to erase individuality or appropriate strength. Example sentences can guide phrasing; they do not supply experiences or commitments to invent. Reflect the position the author has chosen for this occasion rather than mechanically imitating a fixed voice.

## Assess polish and distinctiveness separately

Doshi and Hauser's short-story experiment reports that higher ratings for individual works can coexist with greater similarity across works. Padmakumar and He also examine reduced diversity under particular model and essay-completion conditions. Neither finding establishes the same outcome for all AI writing or all models. [A04, A13]

**This package's proposal:** Identify the author's specific question, observation, and axis of comparison before polishing the prose. If alternatives are useful, compare structures that open different judgments first, rather than merely changing adjectives or style. Check whether stock statements of importance, generic background, or familiar conclusions have replaced the author's own question. Do not assert a long-term loss of creativity.

## Distinguish acknowledgment from agreement

Jakesch and colleagues' experiment finds that writing assistance configured with an opinion affected positions in the text and subsequent self-reported attitudes. Sharma and colleagues examine failures in which the models of that period shifted evaluations or answers toward users' preferences or objections. [A05, A09]

**This package's proposal:** Receiving an intention, as in "You want to examine that point," differs from agreeing that the claim is correct. Critique against criteria and evidence, retaining warranted objections. Also avoid inventing disagreement for the sake of critique. If evaluative language, confidence, support or opposition, or an implicit recommendation changes during revision, check whether the request called for that change.

Hohenstein and colleagues' smart-reply research shows that emotional expression and interpersonal evaluations can also change. [A08] When adding warmth, check whether reservation became approval, thanks became praise, or a factual report became an expression of delight.

## Distinguish designing persuasion from measuring its effects

In Salvi and colleagues' short-debate experiment, GPT-4 using personal information was more persuasive than the human baseline condition. However, the authors' correction of 2026-09-03 states that the direct comparison of personalized and nonpersonalized GPT-4 was not statistically significant. [A10]

**This package's proposal:** When asked for persuasion, design a structure that lets recipients assess reasons, responses to objections, and conditions on the claim. Do not turn a request for explanation or exploration into a task of changing attitudes. What writing enables a reader to do must not always narrow to agreeing with the author.

An LLM playing a reader can identify possible problems with context or implication; it does not measure human responses after reading. When human responses are available, select observations appropriate to the purpose:

- **Understanding content:** Can readers restate the claim and conditions, or correctly distinguish examples?
- **Recognizing the act:** Do they receive the text as reporting a result, proposing a hypothesis, requesting consideration, or another act?
- **Evaluation and response:** Which reasons do they accept or reject, and which questions remain?
- **Interest or emotion:** How do they report responding? Distinguish this from predictions based on the text.

A favorable assessment by one reader or model does not guarantee general effects. Ordinary revision need not require a large study of effects. Report the scope of the checks actually available.

## Check relational expressions in Japanese

Sekizawa and Yanaka evaluate honorific comprehension and conversion using interpersonal relationships and syntax, reporting failures in complex structures and differences among native speakers' judgments. [A07]

**This package's proposal:** Identify the speaker, recipient, agent of the action, people mentioned, and speaker within a quotation. Do not add verbs such as "say" or "think" merely to introduce more honorifics and thereby change who said what. Assess grammatical consistency of honorifics separately from the distance or directness appropriate to the situation. Do not turn this into a rule that Japanese must always be indirect or highly deferential.
