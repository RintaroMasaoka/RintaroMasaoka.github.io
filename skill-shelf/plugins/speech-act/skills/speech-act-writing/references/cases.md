# Cases and Boundaries

The following fictional examples were created for this package. They are not examples from the literature or experimental findings. Examine the correspondence among content, act, and context rather than reusing the wording of the revisions.

## 1. From a List of Facts to a Comparative Question

**Input materials:** Approximation A concerns equilibrium states. The present calculation concerns nonequilibrium states of the same model. Results have not yet been provided. Readers know the distinction between equilibrium and nonequilibrium.

Original:

> Approximation A treats equilibrium states. This study treats nonequilibrium states. The calculation uses method B.

Candidate structure:

> Does approximation A, which concerns equilibrium states, remain valid for nonequilibrium states? This study uses method B to calculate nonequilibrium states of the same model and examine the domain of applicability of approximation A.

Change in the act: Make the difference in the objects of study into a comparative question, rather than merely reporting the difference. However, check whether "examine the domain of applicability" is the author's actual purpose. If the study merely calculates a different quantity, this candidate invents a purpose and cannot be adopted. The input also does not support "previous studies failed" or "we explain this for the first time."

## 2. Match the Force of an Assertion to the Evidence

**Input materials:** A single measurement with uncertainty revealed a trend consistent with mechanism C. Other mechanisms have not been excluded.

Original:

> This result proves mechanism C.

Candidate:

> The trend observed in this measurement is consistent with mechanism C. However, this result alone cannot exclude other mechanisms.

Change in the act: Move from an assertion asking readers to accept C as established to a report that enables them to consider C as a candidate. Give readers what was observed and what remains indistinguishable, rather than merely repeating expressions of possibility.

**Boundary:** "Proves" can be appropriate when a rigorous proof is given under sufficient assumptions. Weakening every assertion undermines the status of a proof. Check scientific validity separately.

## 3. State the Conclusion First

**Input materials:** There is a theorem about bijections on a finite set. Its assumptions and conclusion are established. The proof is specified to appear in a later section.

Poor diagnosis:

> Readers do not yet know the proof, so the theorem should be moved to the end.

Appropriate design: State the theorem first with the necessary assumptions and make clear what the later section will prove. The act of giving readers a view of the conclusion and the act of supplying reasons for it can occur at different times. A preface saying "details follow later" does not by itself repair missing assumptions or an unclear meaning of the conclusion.

Check: From the first part alone, can readers distinguish "what was reported to hold" from "which reasons have not yet been given"? Do not use knowledge of the later proof as the basis for the reading.

## 4. Respond to an Objection

**Input materials:** A reviewer pointed out that the conditions under which an approximation applies were unclear in the text. Those conditions have been added. No new numerical experiments have been performed.

Original response:

> Thank you for your comment. This method is widely used and is valid.

Candidate:

> As you point out, the original text did not specify the conditions under which the approximation applies. We have added those conditions in Section 2 and restricted the conclusions of the paper to that scope.

Change in the act: Move from thanks and a general justification to acknowledging the target of the criticism and reporting the response and its limits. Check that "restricted" describes the actual change. If doubts about validity itself remain, do not say that adding text completed the validation.

**Boundary:** There is no need to agree when the reviewer's premise is mistaken. Choose the act of objecting by identifying the error and its evidence. Politeness and agreement are distinct.

## 5. Create Surprise or Tension

**Input materials:** A symmetry is preserved, yet an observable differs from a simple expectation. Conditions and numerical values have been checked. The author wants to use this discrepancy as motivation in the introduction.

Weak form:

> We obtained a surprising, revolutionary result.

Candidate design: Present the behavior expected from the symmetry to the extent needed, then set that expectation alongside the discrepancy in the verified observation. Next, open the question of which conditions or mechanisms can explain the discrepancy. Use the values and mechanisms in the materials.

Change in the act: Provide the comparison from which surprise might arise, rather than handing readers an evaluation that tells them to be surprised. Whether they are surprised depends on the readers. There is no need to exclude emotion or to exaggerate it. Hiding the conditions under which the expectation holds changes both the content and the sincerity of the act.

## 6. Context Changes the Force of Vocabulary

> In this approximation, we neglect the boundary term.

In a section introducing assumptions, this can be read as a declaration of an operation to be performed. In a section discussing validity, it may be received as a report that neglecting the term has been justified. Check the section and what has already been shown. Replacing it with "the boundary term can be neglected" adds a claim of justification; it is not merely a change to more natural wording.

Japanese particles *wa* and *ga*, subject omission, and sentence endings such as *to kangaeru* ("consider" or "think") and *de aru* (a declarative copula) are not commands with fixed psychological effects. Read focus, agency, and certainty in the actual context. Do not treat principles of English sequence as correct Japanese usage without adaptation.

## 7. Preserve a Text That Already Works

**Input:** Readers are experts. The purpose is a list of data for reuse. Column names, units, conditions, and sources are clear, and values can be located. The request is only to correct typos.

Decision: This falls outside the skill's selection conditions. Make the requested correction without adding a story, direct address, emotion, or a research gap. A list can already function as an act enabling search and comparison.

Even within a review that falls within scope, clear definitions and concise factual reports can remain as they are. Writing propositions is not evidence of a defect.
