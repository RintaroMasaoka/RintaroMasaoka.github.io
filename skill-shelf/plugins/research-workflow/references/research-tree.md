# Research memory

Use the smallest structure that preserves current understanding and reproducible evidence. This is not an authority hierarchy.

- `research/focus.md`: current user goal, scope/assumptions, live gap, next discriminating check and links. Replace stale current-state prose; do not append every session's narrative here.
- One owning note per useful result: claim, derivation, assumptions, limits and links to checks/code/data. Existing `_materials/analyses` or `findings` paths may remain; do not duplicate a proof to satisfy both conventions.
- `research/verification.jsonl`: method-specific checks recorded by `research_memory.py`; record fields and freshness do not certify truth.
- `research/attempts.jsonl`: compact question/method/result/witness history for resource and stagnation decisions. Do not copy complete tool traces.
- Code, data and source files: keep reproducible inputs at their owning paths.

Existing state/map/plan/findings/backlog/guide/checks/logs remain readable history. Do not initialize or synchronize all of them for each session. Update a legacy surface only when its stale content would mislead an active decision; otherwise link to the current focus/result. No mandatory whole-tree sweep or mass migration.

The lead may create, update, reorganize and mark results as superseded or retracted within authorized project scope. For consequential changes, identify the scientific claim and affected dependents, preserve the previous evidence and record the new check. Scripts handle paths, freshness and mechanical propagation; a lower-tier worker may handle bounded ambiguous maintenance. There is no curator role and no separate admission ceremony.

Stable/closed labels describe the status of a question, not verified truth. Record evidence and uncertainty explicitly under [verification](verification.md). An AI review, formal check, numerical experiment and source audit retain their different scope. Directory placement does not change that scope.
